# Methods: formulas and data used by WindGuard TX

This file lists every calculation the app makes, with its source. `docs/RESEARCH.md` holds the practice-by-practice evidence behind the recommendations. The same tables are in the app's Methods sheet (page icon on the map).

## 0. Data sources

"Direct" means the app downloads from the agency that produces the data. Every default source is direct; Open-Meteo is only a backup (hourly wind after 20 s without an answer from NASA POWER, daily weather when gridMET and NASA POWER both fail, elevation when USGS fails) or the ECMWF option.

## 1. Bare-soil wind-erosion potential (Overview risk card, statewide map)

The app computes Wind Erosion Equation (WEQ) step E3 for a bare, smooth (K = 1), wide, unsheltered field:

**E = I × C / 100** (t/ac/yr)

- **I**, soil erodibility index, comes from SSURGO `component.weg` / `component.wei` (NSSH Part 618, §618.93). When an edit to the surface layer changes what the NSSH texture rules give, the app uses the rules' group (§2); otherwise it keeps the survey's.
- **C**, climatic factor, is the official NRCS value from the SSURGO "Climate Factor" land-area overlap for the map unit. For example: Dawson 70, Lubbock 70, Potter 90, Gaines 80, Ward 130, El Paso 130, Harris 10.
  - The definition is C = 34.483 v³ / PE² (Chepil et al. 1962). v is the mean annual wind in mph at 30 ft. PE is the Thornthwaite P–E index, Σ 115 [P/(T−10)]^(10/9), with P in inches (minimum 0.5) and T in °F (minimum 28.4). Garden City, KS = 100.
- **Erodibility index** = C·I/T. T is the SSURGO tolerable soil loss. A value ≥ 8 meets the USDA highly-erodible-land wind criterion (7 CFR 12.21), with C applied as a fraction.

**Risk classes** (t/ac/yr):

| Low | Moderate | High | Very high | Extreme |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 10 | 10–40 | 40–80 | 80–150 | ≥ 150 |

The class edges correspond to roughly 2, 8, 16 and 30 times T = 5. They are app conventions, not NRCS classes. The risk card shows all five classes under the headline with the site's class lit, and says that E is an amount (t/ac/yr), not a score. The exact E is classed in every display unit; where rounding would carry a value across an edge it is shown with one more decimal (39.56 t/ac/yr reads 39.6, Moderate, not 40). In t/ha/yr the edges read 22.4, 90, 179.3 and 336.3: an edge label is a whole number unless rounding would lower it.

**Scales of the other numbers on screen** (each is shown next to its value):

| Number | Scale | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Wind Erodibility Group (WEG) | 1, 2, 3, 4, 4L, 5, 6, 7, 8 | 1 is the most erodible, 8 is not erodible; 3, 4 and 4L share I = 86, and 4L holds calcareous (limy) loamy soils (§2) |
| Wind erodibility index I | 0–310 t/ac/yr | set by the WEG: the yearly loss from a wide, bare, smooth field in the climate of Garden City, KS |
| Climatic factor C | % of Garden City, KS (= 100); Texas counties 4–150 | higher is windier or drier |
| Tolerable soil loss T | 1–5 t/ac/yr | the most a soil can lose each year without losing productivity |
| Erodibility index EI | 0 and up; ≥ 8 is highly erodible land | C·I/T |
| Erosive wind hours | 0–8,760 h/yr | hours with 10 m wind at or above the threshold |

**Statewide map** (`tools/build_layers.py`): SoilWeb's 800 m WEI grid, which is derived from SSURGO, multiplied by the county NRCS C. The result is reprojected to Web Mercator at 1 km resolution and clipped to Texas.

## 2. Wind Erodibility Groups

**NRCS's texture guide to the groups** (National Soil Survey Handbook, Part 618 Subpart B, §618.93, amended August 2024). The Soil tab shows this table with the site's group marked; the Methods sheet shows it in full.

| WEG | Surface layer | I (t/ac/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Very fine sand, fine sand, sand, coarse sand | 160 (coarse sand) to 310 (very fine sand); 220 on average |
| 2 | Loamy very fine sand, loamy fine sand, loamy sand, loamy coarse sand; very fine sandy loam and silt loam with ≤ 5% clay; sapric material (except Folists) | 134 |
| 3 | Very fine sandy loam (not WEG 2), fine sandy loam, sandy loam, coarse sandy loam; noncalcareous silt loam with 20 to < 50% very fine sand and 5 to < 12% clay | 86 |
| 4 | Clay, silty clay; noncalcareous clay loam and silty clay loam with > 35% clay; none with sesquic, parasesquic, ferritic, ferruginous or kaolinitic (iron-rich) mineralogy | 86 |
| 4L | Calcareous loam, silt loam, silt, sandy clay, sandy clay loam, clay loam, silty clay loam | 86 |
| 5 | Noncalcareous loam with < 20% clay; noncalcareous silt loam with 5 to < 20% clay (not WEG 3); noncalcareous sandy clay loam and sandy clay; hemic material | 56 |
| 6 | Noncalcareous loam and silt loam with ≥ 20% clay; noncalcareous clay loam and silty clay loam with ≤ 35% clay; iron-rich silt loam | 48 |
| 7 | Noncalcareous silt; iron-rich noncalcareous silty clay, silty clay loam and clay in Oxisols or Ultisols; fibric material | 38 |
| 8 | Not susceptible to wind erosion because of rock or pararock fragments at the surface and/or wetness; Folists | 0 |

For a named soil, NRCS uses the group recorded in the soil survey; the texture guide is for soils that are not identified (National Agronomy Manual, Part 502: "For most NRCS uses, the I value is assigned for named soils based on wind erodibility groups"). A soil's recorded group can differ from what its texture alone suggests. Pullman clay loam is WEG 7 in 40 of 45 Texas survey records, and Randall clay in playas is WEG 7 too, though by texture they would be 6 and 4. For a soil you have not edited, the app shows the survey's group. It labels a soil's group only by its number and I, never by a texture name; texture names appear only in the class table, as the guide.

**How the app assigns the group from edited values.** The app keeps the survey's group unless your values change what the texture rules below give, so a pH edit leaves Pullman clay loam in WEG 7. The texture class comes from the USDA triangle. The survey's sand-size modifier (e.g. FSL) is kept when the family is unchanged.

| WEG | Surface texture rule | I |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | VFS, FS, S, COS | 310 / 250 / 220 / 160 |
| 2 | Loamy sands; VFSL or SIL with ≤ 5% clay | 134 |
| 3 | Sandy loams; noncalcareous SIL with 20–50% very fine sand and 5–12% clay | 86 |
| 4 | C, SIC; noncalcareous CL/SICL > 35% clay | 86 |
| 4L | Calcareous L, SIL, SI, SC, SCL, CL, SICL | 86 |
| 5 | Noncalcareous L < 20% clay; SIL 5–20% clay; SCL; SC | 56 |
| 6 | Noncalcareous L/SIL ≥ 20% clay; CL/SICL ≤ 35% clay | 48 |
| 7 | Noncalcareous SI | 38 |
| 8 | Through the fragment rule: > 60% rock fragments by volume (not sands or loamy sands), or WEG 6 with ≥ 35% or WEG 7 with ≥ 15% (the next lower I after 48 and 38 is 0) | 0 |

- **RWEQ cross-checks** (Soil tab; Fryrear et al. 1994, 1998):
  - Erodible fraction: EF = (29.09 + 0.31 Sa + 0.17 Si + 0.33 Sa/Cl − 2.59 OM − 0.95 CaCO₃)/100, the fraction of dry aggregates < 0.84 mm.
  - Soil crust factor: SCF = 1/(1 + 0.0066 Cl² + 0.021 OM²).
  - All inputs are in %. Values are flagged when the soil is outside the fitted range: Sa 5.5–93.6, Si 0.5–69.5, Cl 5–39.3, Sa/Cl 1.2–53, OM 0.18–4.79, CaCO₃ 0–25.2.
  - The RWEQ weather factor and transport-capacity equations are listed in the Methods sheet for reference but not computed. They need field length, roughness and cover.
- "Calcareous" in the NSSH means strong or violent effervescence. The app uses CaCO₃ ≥ 5% as a proxy, because the NSSH gives no CaCO₃ threshold.
- Coarse fragments move the group toward less erodible: one group at 15–35% by volume, two groups at 35–60%, and I = 0 above 60%. Sands and loamy sands move fewer groups (none at 15–35%, one at 35–60%, three above 60%). One group means the next lower I (160 → 134, 86 → 56), so 3, 4 and 4L all move to 5.
  - The volume is SSURGO's `fragvoltot_r` when the survey gives it. Otherwise the app takes the whole-soil weight (`frag3to10_r` + `fraggt10_r` for cobbles and stones, plus 100 − % passing the No. 10 sieve for the rest of the soil) and converts it with the layer's bulk density and 2.65 g/cm³ for rock: 17% by weight is about 10% by volume.
  - A texture modifier sets a floor at the middle of its class: gravelly, cobbly, stony, bouldery, channery, flaggy or cherty 15–35%, "very" (e.g. STV) 35–60%, "extremely" (e.g. CBX) 60–90%.
- Not applied, because the survey's own group already covers them for an unedited soil: iron-rich mineralogy, andic material, wetness and Folists.

## 3. Wind climatology at the point (Wind tab)

**Period:** the last 5, 10 or 20 complete calendar years, or your own dates. MERRA-2 runs from 2001 and ECMWF from 1979, both up to a few days ago (about 8 and 3 days). Your own dates need 30 days or more inside the chosen model's record, with 20 or more of them in one calendar month; dates partly outside the record are cut to it, with a message, and fewer than 30 days inside it are refused. A shared or typed link whose dates cannot be analysed says why and shows the last 10 years; dates that only the other model holds switch to it. A day runs midnight to midnight CST (UTC−6). Months are averaged by weight, so an uneven period does not tilt the year: a month needs 480 hours or more to count, yearly totals are shown only when all 12 months are covered, a calendar year counts as complete at 90% of its hours, and the trend needs 5 complete years. Calibration trains on at least 3 years around the period, inside the model's record (widened forward when the record starts too late). The page address keeps the period (`&from=YYYY-MM-DD&to=YYYY-MM-DD`, or `&period=5` or `20`) and, when the model is not MERRA-2, `&model=ecmwf`, so a shared link redoes the same analysis.

**If the chosen model fails,** the other one analyses the same dates when its record holds them. Otherwise a preset period is worked out again for it (and the airport looked up again), and custom dates are cut to its record; with fewer than 30 days left, or no month with 20 days, the first model's error is shown.

**Data:** for the chosen period:
- **Hourly 10 m wind speed and direction.** You choose the gridded model on the Wind tab, and each model is the other's automatic fallback: when the chosen source returns an error or has not started answering within 20 s (both normally answer in 1–3 s), the app switches; once data is arriving, a slow download is allowed up to 90 s.
  - **NASA MERRA-2 (default):** the NASA POWER hourly API, direct, UTC, at 0.5° × 0.625°. It has no gust variable.
  - **ECMWF (backup or option):** the Open-Meteo archive `best_match`, which is ECMWF IFS analysis at 9 km from 2017 on and ERA5 at 0.25° (~28 km) before 2017. ERA5-Land does not supply wind here. Daily max gust comes with it.
- **Daily precipitation, grass reference ET₀ (ASCE Penman–Monteith) and Tmax/Tmin:** gridMET over OPeNDAP, one 4 km cell. Scale factor, offset and fill value are read from the dataset attributes. The first fallback is NASA POWER daily (direct): precipitation, temperature, dew point, 2 m wind and all-sky solar radiation, from which the app computes FAO-56 Penman–Monteith ET₀ (Allen et al. 1998, Eqs. 6–21 and 37–40; albedo 0.23, G = 0; Hargreaves on days missing an input). The last fallback is Open-Meteo daily precipitation, FAO-56 ET₀ and mean temperature.
- **Daily max wind** comes from the hourly series.
- **Open-Meteo cost:** the default lookup makes no Open-Meteo calls. If you pick ECMWF, a 10-year lookup costs about 130 Open-Meteo "calls" (78 at the site plus 52 at the station), against free limits of 600 per minute and 10,000 per day per IP.

**Calibration to the nearest station (default):**
- **Station data:** routine hourly METARs (FM-15: 2-minute mean wind at 10 m, plus gusts) from NOAA NCEI GHCNh, one request per year. The report nearest each whole hour is kept, and NCEI quality codes 2, 3, 6 and 7 (suspect or erroneous) are dropped. Stations without a GHCNh id, or with GHCNh coverage below the threshold, use the Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS archive.
- **Quality control:** sustained wind > 40 m/s or gust > 55 m/s is dropped. Hobbs, for example, has 250-kt garbled reports that otherwise dominate cubic energy sums.
- **Station choice:** the nearest airport within 150 km with ≥ 60% of hours over its training window and over the period. NWS/FAA ASOS sites rank ahead of AWOS by a 100 km handicap, because in West Texas several AWOS anemometers (Seminole, Andrews, Snyder, Pecos) read 40–60% fewer erosive hours than nearby ASOS. You can pick another station in the Wind tab.
- **Method:** monthly empirical quantile mapping. It is trained on the same gridded model at the station's grid cell versus the station on the same hours, using 101 quantiles between 0.1% and 99.9%, with end ratios beyond those. It is then applied to reanalysis at the site, so the model's site-vs-station difference is kept. Daily max gusts are mapped the same way against station daily max gusts; with MERRA-2, which has no gusts, the model's daily max hourly wind is mapped onto station gusts. Direction and timing stay from the model.
- **Other sources you can select:** "Airport station only" (rain and ET₀ still from gridMET) and "Gridded model only".
- **Check, Lubbock 2016–2025:** reanalysis vs LBB mean wind 4.41 vs 5.24 m/s, hourly r = 0.80, RMSE 1.85 m/s. Erosive hours (≥ 8 m/s) were 5.3% vs 15.1%.
  - Calibrated: 1,304 erosive h/yr vs the station's 1,321 (reanalysis 451).
  - High-risk months: Feb–May calibrated and station, Jan–Apr reanalysis.
  - Drift potential: 553 VU calibrated vs 153 VU reanalysis.

With gridMET moisture, the Lubbock calibrated high-risk months are Feb–Apr. May falls just under the cutoff (9.3% of annual risk), because gridMET records more May rain (77 mm) than the ECMWF model did.

**West Texas Mesonet towers (switched off).** Built in, but off because the mesonet's data policy forbids public use without its permission. `ENABLE_WTM` in `js/wind.js` turns them on. For a local test, `?wtm=1` turns them on when the page is served from the same computer (localhost or a file); the public site ignores it. When on:
- **Data:** the hourly NWS SHEF reports (WFO Lubbock product RR5LUB) archived by the Iowa Environmental Mesonet, read with `hads.py`, one station-year per request, one request at a time (IEM refuses a `hads.py` request that starts within about a second of another from the same address; failed IEM requests, including the airport fallback, are retried twice). Speed is column `USIRZZ` (from October 2021 `USIRZZZ`) in whole mph, direction `UDIRZZ`/`UDIRZZZ`; values are stamped at :00 UTC. The feed has no gusts and no QC flags. Tower names and positions come from the NWS station list that IEM publishes; the mesonet's own site list only tells which stations are its towers.
- **Years:** the latest 5 complete years (one year takes about 11 s; the archive starts in December 2010). A tower therefore needs 5 complete years in the archive (132 of the 189 towers in 2026), and it can calibrate the 20-year period too.
- **QC:** speed 0–40 m/s; runs of 6+ hours at one non-zero speed (stuck sensor) and 3+ calm hours in a row (iced cups; the towers are calm 0.2–0.9% of hours) are dropped; ≥ 60% of hours, or 20% for a station you pick. Towers with too little data are passed over.
- **Ranking:** distance + 0 km for ASOS, + 20 km for a tower, + 100 km for AWOS. So a tower ranks ahead of an ASOS airport when it is more than 20 km closer, and ahead of an AWOS airport unless it is more than 80 km farther. The airport result appears first. When a tower ranks ahead of every airport, it loads alongside, and about a minute later its speed calibration replaces the airport's if it passes the exposure check. Daily gusts stay mapped to the airport; with no airport within 150 km, or none with a model series, they are n/a. "Airport station only" stays the airport, because a tower covers only 5 years.
- **Exposure check:** airports and towers should both stand on open ground. Over the tower's 5 years the app compares the tower with the airport it found, each relative to the model at its own cell: the ratio of hours ≥ 8 m/s and of mean wind. A tower with less than 0.85 times the airport's erosive hours is taken as sheltered and skipped, and so is a tower that cannot be compared (the airport has no model series, or too few shared hours). A tower is used without the check only when no airport was found. After a skip the app checks one more tower that still ranks ahead of the airport, then keeps the airport calibration. A station you pick yourself is used anyway, and the card shows its check. The check trusts the airport, so it is weaker when that airport is an AWOS. In the app, Plainview 1S (PVST2) against LBB, 2021–2025, gave 0.51 of the erosive hours and 0.79 of the mean wind and was skipped; Aiken (AIKT2) then gave 0.89 and 0.95 and was used.

**Erosive threshold:** 8 m/s at 10 m by default. This is the NRCS figure of 13 mph at 1 ft, or about 18 mph at 30 ft (Lyles & Krauss 1971; NAM Part 502). You can change it from 5 to 12 m/s.

**Monthly metrics:**
- Erosive hours: hours with u ≥ uₜ.
- Erosive energy: Σ u²(u − uₜ).
- Dryness: D = clamp((ET₀ − P)/ET₀, 0, 1), with P and ET₀ from gridMET. If no moisture data loads, D = 1 and months rank by wind energy alone.
- **Risk index** = energy × D. A month's **share** is its index divided by the annual total.
  - **Red** (high risk): share ≥ 10% (vs. 8.3% if spread evenly).
  - **Amber**: share ≥ 6.7%.
  - These cut-offs are an app convention.
  - Red, amber and high-risk months are given only for a period covering all 12 months. With fewer, the shares are of the months covered, no month is ranked (they show gray), and Practices uses the typical Feb–May windy season, labelled as such.

**Gust days:** daily max gust ≥ 17.9 m/s (40 mph). Shown as n/a for uncalibrated MERRA-2, and for a tower calibration with no airport within 150 km.

**FAO climatic index:** (1/100) Σ u₂³ · D · days. u₂ = 0.748 u₁₀ (FAO-56 Eq. 47). Months with P > ET₀ are clamped to 0.

**Fryberger & Dean (1979) drift potential:** DP = Σ V²(V − 12)/N, with V in knots at 10 m over all hours N.
- RDP is the vector resultant of those terms in the downwind direction; RDD is its direction.
- DP classes: low < 200, intermediate 200–399, high ≥ 400 VU.
- RDP/DP: multidirectional < 0.3, intermediate < 0.8, unidirectional ≥ 0.8.

**Prevailing erosive wind** is the 22.5° sector carrying the most erosive energy Σu²(u − uₜ), smoothed with half weight on its neighbors. Barrier and strip orientation is that direction plus 90°. There is none when no hour reaches the threshold.

**Trend:** ordinary least-squares slope of erosive hours per year, with a two-sided t-test at 95%.

Airport anemometers sit on open, mown ground (mesonet towers should too; the exposure check tests it), so calibrated values describe exposed fields. Sheltered sites blow less.

## 4. Soil profile (Soil tab and exports)

**Source:** SSURGO via Soil Data Access.
- Tables: mapunit, legend, sacatalog, muaggatt, component, chorizon, chtexturegrp (RV), corestrictions, coecoclass, cocropyld, coeplants, muaoverlap/laoverlap (MLRA, C, R).
- The "dominant soil" is the largest component that has horizon data.

**Crop-model parameters** (DSSAT conventions; Romero et al. 2012; DSSAT CSM `SOILDYN.for`):

| Parameter | Rule |
|---|---|
| LL, DUL | `wfifteenbar_r`/100, `wthirdbar_r`/100 |
| SAT | `wsatiated_r`/100, else Saxton–Rawls θS, else 1 − BD/2.65; always ≥ DUL + 0.01 |
| SSKS (cm/h) | `ksat_r` (µm/s) × 0.36 |
| SLOC | OM/1.724 |
| SLNI | SLOC/10 per horizon, capped at 0.5%; a merged or custom layer takes its horizons' mean by mass |
| SLCF | coarse fragments > 2 mm, % by weight of the whole soil: `frag3to10_r` + `fraggt10_r` plus the gravel the No. 10 sieve holds back |
| SRGF | 1 for layers ending ≤ 15 cm, else exp(−0.02 × mid-depth); 0 below a restrictive layer |
| SLDR | By drainage class: very poorly and subaqueous 0.01, poorly 0.05, somewhat poorly 0.25, moderately well 0.40, well 0.60, somewhat excessively 0.75, excessively 0.85 |
| SLRO | Curve number by hydrologic group (the second letter of a dual group) and slope class (0–2, 2–5, 5–10, > 10%): A 61/64/68/71, B 73/76/80/83, C 81/84/88/91, D 84/87/91/94 |
| SLU1 | 4 mm for sand and loamy sand, 8 for clay, silty clay and sandy clay, 6 for the other classes (the HC27 generic soils of the DSSAT SoilGrids profiles, Han et al. 2019; CSM's default is 6) |
| SALB | SSURGO `albedodry_r` |

**Edited surface layer, and blanks in your own soil:** water retention, bulk density and Ksat come from Saxton & Rawls (2006), using sand and clay fractions and OM % capped at 8. The estimates are kept physical for any input: wilting point ≥ 0.01 cm³/cm³, field capacity ≥ wilting point + 0.01, saturation ≥ field capacity + 0.01, with bulk density (1 − θS) × 2.65 and Ksat from the bounded values; sand and clay that add up to more than 100 % are scaled to 100 %.

The Soil tab's DSSAT download and the data sheet use the same writer (§7).

**Slope and aspect** come from USGS 3DEP (1–10 m lidar/DEM) through the Elevation Point Query Service: elevation at 5 points about 100 m apart. slope = 100·√(gx² + gy²), aspect = atan2(−gx, −gy). Fallback: Copernicus 90 m DEM via Open-Meteo.

## 5. Field or area analysis (area tool) and the map-click check

**Boundary:** drawn on the map (tap the corners; tap the first corner, double-click or Finish to close) or uploaded. Zipped shapefiles and loose .shp/.dbf/.prj files are read with shpjs, which reprojects using the .prj; KML and KMZ with togeojson (and JSZip for KMZ); GeoJSON directly. Everything is read in the browser. Every polygon in the file counts; lines and points are ignored. Limit 500 km² (123,550 ac). Area is the spherical polygon area (WGS84 radius).

**Soil polygons:** one Soil Data Access query clips the SSURGO map-unit polygons to the boundary (`mupolygongeo.STIntersection(boundary)`, with the boundary simplified to at most 600 vertices). Two more queries read each map unit's components (WEG, I, %) and its climatic-factor area (C).

**Left out** (when "Leave out buildings, roads and water" is ticked):
- **Buildings:** FEMA/ORNL USA Structures footprints.
- **Roads:** Census TIGER primary, secondary and local roads, as a band around the centreline. Half-widths by MTFCC class: S1100 15 m, S1200 9 m, S1400 5 m, S1630 6 m, S1640 4 m, S1730/S1740/S1750 3 m, S1500 2 m, paths 1.5 m, others 4 m.
- **Water:** USGS NHD water bodies of type lake/pond (390), reservoir (436), swamp/marsh (466) and estuary (493). Playas (361) stay in because most are farmed. Esri's copy of NHD is asked at the same time and used if NHD has not answered within 5 s. SSURGO map units named "Water" are always left out.
- Points outside the Texas outline or without soil data.

**Shares:** about 20,000 stratified random points (one per grid cell, seeded so the same area gives the same answer) are tested against the boundary, the left-out features and the soil polygons. Each kept point adds each component's percentage (÷ 100) to that component's WEG and to its risk class, with E = I × C / 100 using the map unit's C. Shares are of the open land in rated components; components without a WEG (rock outcrop, urban land, pits) are reported separately. Acres = share × analysed acres. The sampling error of a 20,000-point share is under ±0.7 percentage points.

**Checks:** a 157 ac field uploaded as GeoJSON, KML, KMZ, a zipped WGS84 shapefile, a zipped UTM 14N shapefile and loose UTM .shp/.dbf/.prj files gave 158 ac every time. A 2 km square on the edge of Shallowater: 826 ac, 77 ac of roads and 76 ac of buildings left out, 66% WEG 6. A 100 km² square in south Lubbock took 6 s.

**Area grid** (`js/grid.js`, `js/gridfiles.js`): the boundary as a grid of cells, with soil and weather in each.
- *Cells:* from 20 cm up. Square, north up or turned by an angle clockwise; hexagons with points or flat sides up, sized by their width across the flats; or your own grid of polygons (cells whose centres are inside the boundary are kept, with your id column). Coordinates: the area's UTM zone (WGS 84, Krüger series, Karney 2011) or CONUS Albers (EPSG:5070, Snyder 1987), both checked against pyproj to under a millimetre. Rows and columns are whole steps from an origin: 0, 0 by default, so grids of one size, angle and origin line up, turned or not; or a corner of a grid you have, as X and Y.
- *How much detail:* finer cells line up with other grids but add no information. The soil survey is mapped at 1:12,000–1:24,000, a map unit mixes soils without showing where each lies, and the weather comes in cells of 4 km or more. The sheet and README say so.
- *A cell's value:* what lies at its centre (polygons are painted on the lattice row by row, so 50 million cells are possible), or weighted by what covers it, from 25 points in the cell's box, up to 200,000 cells; there a code (map unit, Wind Erodibility Group, weather cell) is the value covering the most, counted by value, as map units can share a group, and every weather column is that one weather cell's. Your own grid is always weighted. Under the centre rule each weather cell is painted with its sides along parallels and meridians (a point every 0.002°) and cells on its edges are looked up by their centre, so every cell takes the weather cell its centre lies in.
- *Soil:* SSURGO components and horizons of the map units in the boundary (Soil Data Access), on the layer bottoms you choose: weighted means (organic matter and CaCO₃ by mass) and harmonic Ksat, as in §7, but a layer below the end of a profile is left blank rather than repeated. Within a map unit, the main soil that has the value, or all its soils weighted by their %. Also the map unit key, Wind Erodibility Group (4L as 41), I, C and E. Cells on buildings, roads and water (§ above), or mostly on them in the weighted rule, are marked in a cover column and have no soil values.
- *Weather:* each grid cell gives the means of its weather cell (gridMET and PRISM 1/24°, NASA POWER 0.5° by 0.625°) over the dates you choose: rain and ET₀ per year or calendar month from the days with data, the rest as means. A number points to its row in `weather/cells.csv`, with a daily table for each weather cell, because a daily series for every grid cell would be far too large.
- *Files:* a GeoTIFF (Float32, Deflate, one band per column with its name, NoData −9999, square cells only; a turned grid carries its turn in a transformation matrix, which some software ignores), a shapefile, GeoJSON and CSV of the cells, `bands.csv` and `soil_units.csv`. Checked with rasterio and geopandas: every pixel's centre is its lattice cell's centre, also when turned, and polygons are valid and tile without overlap, with outer rings clockwise in the shapefile and counter-clockwise in GeoJSON (RFC 7946), holes the other way, whatever the winding of your own grid. Hexagons can only be polygons. A shapefile holds 240 columns; with more, it is not offered, and Build is refused while no ticked file can be made. Limits: 50 million cells in the area's box, 1.5 billion values in a GeoTIFF, 500,000 polygons in a shapefile, 100,000 in GeoJSON, 1 million CSV rows.

**Map-click check:** before a clicked point (or a dragged pin) is analysed, the same three layers are read for a ~180 m box around it. If the point falls inside a building or water body, or within a road's half-width, it is refused with the reason. Typed coordinates, GPS and place search are not checked. If the services don't answer within 3.5 s the point is accepted.

## 6. Recommendations (Practices tab)

The rules are encoded in `js/practices.js` from `docs/RESEARCH.md`. Key thresholds:

- **Cover targets:** ≥ 60% surface cover on WEG 1–2, ≥ 40% on WEG 3–4L, ≥ 30% otherwise.
- **Flat-cover erosion reduction:** 10% → 35%, 30% → 70%, 50% → 85%, 70% → 93% (NebGuide G1537).
- **Residue table:** NAM Exhibit 502-1 E-table ratios (I 86, C 100, wide field), scaled to the site's E. This is approximate.
- **Cover crops:** 30 lb/ac rye or wheat on dryland; terminate 6–8 weeks before cotton (dryland) or 2–4 weeks (irrigated). Source: AgriLife Lamesa/Lubbock trials.
- **Barriers:** ≥ 1.5 ft tall, 40–50% porous, ≤ 10H apart (TX 603). Trap strips ≥ 15 ft wide (589C). Ridges ≤ 4× height apart (588). Windbreak protects 10H leeward and 2H windward (TX 380).
- **Grazing:** 25% harvest efficiency (≤ 33% in lesser prairie-chicken range). Drought checkpoints Apr 1, Jun 15, Jul 15, Aug 15, Nov 1 (TX 528).
- **Seeding:** Feb 1/15–May 15, or the monsoon in the Trans-Pecos. Drill rows ≤ 12 in, ¼–½ in deep. Defer grazing ≥ 1 growing season (TX 550). TxDOT 2024 Item 164 district mixes; University Lands Permian mix.
- **Shinnery and sand sagebrush:** tebuthiuron about 0.3 lb a.i./ac; treat ≤ 50% of the property per 2 years; 150-ft dune buffer; defer grazing 2 years (WAFWA).

## 7. Model files and downloads (data sheet)

The data sheet writes files for one location and a chosen date range. Every layout below was checked against the model's manual, its official example files and the reading code of its source (DSSAT-CSM 4.8.6 `IPWTH`/`IPSOIL`; SWAT+ rev 62 `cli_*`/`soil_db_read`; WXPM3020 and the EPIC/APEX user manuals; ApsimX `ApsimTextFile`, `Weather` and `Soil.Check`; AquaCrop 7.3 `LoadClim`/`LoadProfile`). A test runs every writer on 20 years of Lubbock weather and a SSURGO profile under each layering choice and passes all those checks. None of the files has been run in the models here. A model or table that cannot be made is replaced by a `NOT_WRITTEN.txt` giving the reason, and the rest of the zip is kept.

**Preview.** The Preview button runs everything a download runs except the zip, and draws what the writers were given: weather (`js/preview.js`), by day, month or year, rain and ET₀ as sums of the days with data and the rest as means, values no station reports left out, with a table of days with data, missing days and ranges, and on request the gap-free copy of §7 *Gaps* over it; the soil layers of each ticked model's file (the functions its writer uses), as bars or as a profile with depth to scale; the hourly wind series by day, month or year and the monthly and annual summaries; and the files, with sizes and first lines, the notes and anything that will not be written. The weather and climate reads are kept, so Download after Preview reads nothing twice. A notice appears when a choice changes after the preview.

**Tabs.** The sheet's tabs make the weather files, the soil files, or both together in one zip: each model's writer runs with what the tab has, so its folder holds that kind of file only. Tables go with the tab they belong to. The Soil tab needs no dates, except that CENTURY's and DayCent's site files hold climate lines too, for which it reads 10 complete years of the source chosen under Weather files.

**Weather sources.** Dates outside the chosen source's record are left out, and the status line and README say so: gridMET runs from 1979 to its last day (about 2 days ago), PRISM from 1981 to about 3 days ago, NASA POWER from 1981 to about 8 days ago, and your file covers its own days unless gaps are filled from gridMET.
- gridMET (default): 9 variables from THREDDS for the chosen days. T = K − 273.15; Rs (MJ/m²/day) = W/m² × 0.0864; vapour pressure from specific humidity, ea = q p / (0.622 + 0.378 q), with p from elevation (FAO-56 Eq. 7); u₂ = 0.748 u₁₀; ET₀ is gridMET's grass reference.
- PRISM: rain, Tmax and Tmin from PRISM's 4 km daily grids through ACIS GridData, grid 21, which is documented and can be read from a web page. PRISM's own file service cannot (no CORS header, and a year would be about 2,555 files and 6.5 GB). ACIS's values match PRISM's raster: on 2023-09-15 at Lubbock's cell 56.2 against 56.17 mm and 25.93 against 25.93 °C, and whole years within rounding. PRISM has no wind, and ACIS no humidity or solar radiation, so those come from gridMET, or NASA POWER when gridMET does not answer, and the status line says so. ET₀ is FAO-56 Penman–Monteith from these (Hargreaves on days without radiation, dew point or wind). Lubbock 2023: 1,880 mm against gridMET's own 1,885, r 0.99.
  - *Dates.* PRISM's day D is the 24 hours ending at 1200 UTC on D, so it is mostly calendar day D−1. For calendar day D the app takes PRISM's date D+1, which lines it up with gridMET's wind and with stations: in 2023, Tmax against seven airports has r 0.993–0.998 that way and 0.88–0.96 as labelled. So the files differ by a day from PRISM's own. The offset is measured, not documented by gridMET.
  - *Limits.* Contiguous US. The last six months are provisional and can change up to eight times. Each README gives the credit PRISM's terms ask for: its name, address and the date read.
  - *Not used.* PRISM's Data Explorer back end also serves dew point, vapour pressure deficit and solar radiation and answers web pages, but it is undocumented, and nothing says other sites may call it.
- NASA POWER daily: ET₀ by FAO-56 Penman–Monteith (Hargreaves, Eq. 52, on days without radiation, dew point or wind).
- Your file: CSV, text or the first sheet of an Excel workbook (read as stored values: real dates, full precision, percentages as 0–100). The delimiter is the file's own (comma, semicolon or tab, with decimal commas where the comma is not the delimiter). Lines above the header row (NASA POWER, Open-Meteo, Campbell TOA5, PRISM, Daymet) are skipped. Units °C/°F, mm/in, MJ/W/kW/langley/kWh, m/s/mph/km/h/kn; wind at any height is moved to 10 and 2 m with the log law (z₀ = 0.03 m). Blank or text cells and codes of −99 or below are missing, and "T" (trace) rain is 0. Vapour pressure comes from the dew point, else from RHmax and RHmin (FAO-56 Eq. 17), else from mean RH (Eq. 19); the dew point follows from it. Gaps can be filled from gridMET for the same days (not ET₀, which would not match your weather). ET₀ is the file's own grass-reference column where it has one (et0, ETo, ETos, reference ET; never ETr or alfalfa-reference, pan evaporation or PET; in the rain unit), else FAO-56 Penman–Monteith from its columns after any filling, with solar radiation from the temperature range (Eq. 50) on days without it and Hargreaves (Eq. 52) on days without humidity or wind. The zip's README counts the days of each.
  - *Templates and units.* The sheet shows the layout and makes a template (`js/templates.js`): tick the columns you have, choose each one's unit, and download it as CSV or Excel. A column's name can state its unit, as the template's do (`tmax (F)`, `Rain_mm_Tot`, `SlrW_Avg`, `srad (kWh/m2/d)`, `WS_ms_Avg`), and a wind column its height (`wind at 3 m (mph)`, `WS10M`, `u2`); that is used for that column, and the choices in the sheet apply only to the others. Humidity of 1 or less on every day, or in a column named as a fraction (`rh (fraction)`, `(0-1)`), is read as a fraction. The sheet says what each column was read in, and notes a value that does not fit its unit (a typical Tmax above 55 °C, solar radiation above 45 MJ/m², wind above 25 m/s, a typical wet day above 100 mm).
  - *Columns* are found by name, tried on the name without units and notes in brackets. An exact name beats a pattern, and either beats a second choice: a column with a duration shorter than a day ("Rain_60min" gives way to "Rain"; a day's, as in "Precip 24 Hour Total", says nothing) or rain named an accumulation or "since" a time. Other measurements under the same words are never read: battery, panel, soil, surface, dew-point and wet-bulb temperatures; times of extremes; rain rates, hourly or weekly amounts and normals; net, longwave and clear-sky radiation; wind directions, gusts, maxima and runs. The data sheet and the README list which header each quantity was read from, so a wrong match can be seen.
  - *Dates* are chosen from the rows: a column of full dates first (YYYY-MM-DD, YYYYMMDD, also with a time run on as YYYYMMDDHHMM, or M/D/YYYY; D/M/YYYY when a first number in the column is above 12), else year, month and day, else year and day of the year, else any other column of full dates. "Day" holds the dates when "Time" holds clock times. A Campbell TOA5 daily record stamped at 00:00 holds the day before (2024-07-02 00:00:00 is 1 July). Impossible dates (30 February, month 13) are left out and noted.
  - *Cells.* The delimiter is the one that makes the same number of columns on most lines and gives a header row, as title lines can outnumber a short table's. A row of units under the header is read, as is a unit written in a column's cells (`40°F`, `0.1 in`); a cell in another unit than its column is a gap. Blank, text, −99 or below, and 999, 9999 and 6999 are missing, except where the column's unit can hold them (9999 kJ/m², 99.9 km/h, or tenths in GHCN's own CSV). GHCN's tenths and Daymet's daylight-mean radiation (with `dayl` in seconds) are recognised. Numbers in scientific notation and with lab flags are read; a cell that still does not read as a number is a gap, and the notes name the column, the count and the first such cell. A row of zeros in every column is a logger's placeholder, so a gap, as is a day whose rows are all such rows.
  - *Rows through the day.* When rows differ in their time on at least half the days, each day is summarised: the highest Tmax and maximum humidity, the lowest Tmin and minimum humidity, the mean of other humidity, dew point and wind, solar radiation added up for an energy and averaged for W/m². Each day's rows are put in time order first, also in a file that runs newest first. A logger stamps each reading at the end of its interval (01:00 to 00:00, or 00:00 to 00:00), so its midnight row belongs to the day before. This is recognised from the file's ends (it starts about one row after midnight, or ends on a midnight row alone on its day) and from its midnight rows: one that repeats the day before's last reading, above 0, while the next row changes says the stamps are at the interval's end; one that changes and goes on into the day, above 0, says they are at its start. Rain says so, and so does a daily solar or ET₀ value repeated on every row of the day before. Both ends are enough unless the midnight rows disagree; one end needs them to agree; with neither, a whole day must hold the value its midnight row repeats, and no midnight row may say the stamps are at the start. On rows through the day, a row of zeros next to a reading within 3 °C of it, before or after, is kept as a calm, dark, freezing hour, unless one of its zeros cannot be a reading: a humidity of 0, solar radiation of 0 next to daylight, or rain of 0 between two wet readings. That last is allowed only in a column of amounts (rain that falls back to 0 between wet rows, eases from one wet row to a smaller one, and falls twice within one day, which a total that resets once a day cannot), and only where the readings on both sides have a high and a low within half a degree of each other, as a real reading's two zeros do. Rain is added up unless it is one of these. A daily value repeated on each row: the same on every reading of each wet day, and on at least half of a usual day's rows on one of them at least; where dry hours are left blank it fills every row of its wet days, and where they are 0 a blank is a missing reading. A value over 300 mm held through each day, far above any change from day to day, is a season total instead. ET₀ and solar energy are read the same way, but a value on fewer than half a day's rows counts as the day's only where, on some day of the file, it is given within an hour of midnight, is more than a flat hourly amount could be (1.5 MJ/m², 0.8 mm), or would add up to more than a day can hold (50 MJ/m², 15 mm); then every day's value is read so. Otherwise hourly daylight amounts are added up. A running total that only rises within each day gives its largest value. It is recognised when it rises within the day on three days or more and most of its wet days are given to their end on their highest reading, or when it is given only when it changes. With fewer such days, it is recognised when its name says so (daily, today, since) or when it holds its value between rains to the end of each wet day. A day left blank before its first tip and then given on every row to its end counts as such a day. On rows a few minutes apart, a reading held three hours is a total in itself, not rain: so a day whose readings stop on a value held three hours also counts, where every wet day that stops early stops within the same hour of the clock (a total since 7 am left blank from its reset to the next tip), and so does one value held three hours from near midnight. The file's first or last day given on one row only (the day before's total) says nothing either way. A running total that resets at another hour (a logger's midnight row still holding yesterday's total, midnight UTC, 7 am) gives what each row rose by, and after a reset all it holds. It is recognised when its name says so, or when it holds its value between rains and falls at one hour of the day. The falls must come once a day, within two hours of the same clock time. At least one must come after a climb in steps, or each after a value held over a midnight; or they must all come within one hour of the same clock time, each after a value held three hours, where the rain before them began at different hours. A fall that comes after rows left blank is timed from the first blank row. A single fall counts after a climb in steps; on hourly rows, also the fall of a total held from the file's first row; on rows minutes apart, only after a value held three hours, or where the file's last reading is held three hours after it and the fall lands on blank rows or the total then climbs in two steps or more, or in one step to a value other than the one it fell from. A season total gives what it rose by each day. On rows through the day, what it rose by over a missing day is dropped: the day after one, like the file's first day, gives only what it rose by over its own rows. It is recognised when it is held over midnights and reset at most once a year (and not within a year of its start from 0 in the file), or when it never falls and is kept from one day to the next: kept so over two midnights or more, with readings on at least two rows of each day, whatever its name; kept over one midnight only, where its name does not say daily. A total that never falls and opens each day far above any rise is one too. A weighing gauge's total wavers. A fall of a tenth or less, or a fall to no less than half that is back to the reading before it within three readings (or is the file's last reading), is a dip, not a reset, and only a rise past the highest reading is rain. If the name says the total is a day's (since 7 AM, Rain Today), only a fall of a tenth or less is a dip; every larger fall is a reset. Daily rows of a season total, which never falls and holds its value over dry days (or stays well above any day's rise), are read the same way, and there the day after a missing one, like the file's first day, is a gap. A fall to less than half is a reset, and only once a year. A smaller fall is a dip: that day gets no rain, and only a rise past the reading before it counts. Each dip of more than one step, or one not back within three days, needs the value to hold from one day to the next more than five times for each; a dip of one step of the column's resolution that is back within three days (or is the last day) needs nothing, where the total rises past its highest somewhere and dips no more than once a week. Some shapes cannot be told apart from the data alone, so the reader takes the reading that is right on most real files: steady drizzle that ends on its highest hour reads as a running total; the same amount on every row for three hours or more on rows minutes apart, as one tip held to a reset; drizzle from a file's first hourly row, as a total carried in from before the file; a reset that lands one step below the total and is back on the next reading, as a gauge's dip; a total kept over a midnight that never falls, as a season total rather than a daily total whose first hour was wetter than the whole day before; pairs of equal daily values that each rise once, as daily amounts; daily values that drop two steps or more and come back, as daily amounts unless the value holds from one day to the next more than five times for each such drop; and a file that runs from 01:00 to a lone 00:00, as stamped at the hour's end unless its midnight rows say otherwise. The notes say which reading was used. A column's name helps where the rows cannot decide. On rows through the day, accumulated, cumulative or since a date (since Oct 1, since 1 Oct, since 10/1, since 2024-10-01) point to a season total, where the name has no day, daily, today, midnight, hour, hrly, minute, 24, other span (1d, P1D, 10m, 07-07, 0700-0700, since last obs) or time of day (since 7 AM, since 0700, since noon, since sunrise or sunset, 12Z, UTC, GMT, LST, local time). The name decides where the rows allow it: the total falls only a year apart. The rows win where it falls once a day at one hour on two days or more, or where every day given runs from its first reading on every row to the day's end and one is blank from the day's start or holds its value while other days are blank: that is a total that starts again each day or at some hour, whatever its name. They win too where a value given on rows in a row, holding (a fall of one or two steps of the column's resolution to more than half, or a 0, ends it; a reset does not), is then left blank, and after the blank rows the total goes on at under half the value held or more than two steps below it, or the file opens no more than ten times above its largest rise from one row to the next and ends on blank rows or opens on them: that is a total that resets at some hour and is left blank while it is 0, so each blank row counts as 0 and the first reading after blank rows adds all it holds (a season total is given on every row or only when it changes, and an outage of one shows neither; nor is a column whose dry hours are written 0 left blank while it is 0). Blank rows of less than a day between two equal readings above 0, where no reset seen in the file comes at that time of day and no higher reading follows within the hour, are an outage of the value held, not 0. In both tests a reading missing counts as given, not as a blank row: up to an hour of blank rows, or two rows where rows are more than half an hour apart, between two equal readings above 0, or up to an hour of blank rows between a reading and a higher one where the column also holds a value on rows in a row somewhere (across a reading missing too), in a column given on rows in a row for at least half its readings, where no reset seen in the file (a fall to under half or by more than two steps, or the blank rows that end it) comes at that time of day. Nothing is filled where the column holds one value only. Readings missing make the rows win by the first test on their own only where the rows rise or fall within a day, no reading after blank rows across a midnight is at or above the one before them (where that one is more than one step of the column's resolution), and none after blank rows is more than ten times the largest rise from one row to the next and more than 75 mm an hour could bring since the row before: otherwise it is a season total kept over an outage, or one whose first rows were lost. Where the rows cannot tell (no fall, one fall, given only when it changes, dry hours blank or 0), the name decides; a column with no reading above 0 is not read by its name. Read so by its name, a fall is a gauge's dip only if it is a step or two of the column's resolution, not to 0, and back within three readings to no more than a step or two above, or on the file's last three rows (or the last three before the blank rows that end it, the fall coming on the row after the reading before it); a day of blank rows is no missing day where the readings never repeat the one before (a total given only when it changes); and where 0s outnumber blank rows, the readings above 0 never repeat the one before and they open more than ten times above any rise, 0s that go back up past the reading before them are dry hours, not falls, and so are the file's first and last 0s (where some 0s went back up so, or where the readings above 0 make one run and 0s follow it); such dry hours are no falls when the rows decide either. A day's value on all its rows stays that day's rain under such a name too, unless a value holds over two days, or the values stay three times above any rise from day to day and never fall. Day, daily, today, since midnight or since a time of day point to a total that starts again each day or at some hour. On daily rows, accumulated, cumulative or since a date (not since a time of day, and without day, today, hour, or 24 outside the date) point to a season total once its value holds over two days. A column named for a season, a year or YTD is not read as rain. These rules were checked on 2024 hourly, 15-minute, 5-minute and daily rain at Lubbock, Tyler and Houston, written in each of these forms, oldest and newest first, in windows of 1 to 60 days. A few repeated days of a daily file keep each column's last value, and exact repeats count once.
  - *Limits.* A Tmax or Tmin outside −60…60 °C, solar radiation below 0.1 or above 50 MJ/m² a day, rain below 0 or above 1,900 mm a day, humidity of 0 or above 105 %, wind above 75 m/s and ET₀ above 50 mm a day are gaps, counted in the notes. When most of Tmax or Tmin are out of range the unit cannot be right, and the file is refused before anything is filled. So is a file with more than one station. YY/MM/DD dates are refused, since they cannot be told from D/M/YY in general.
  - *Notes* on how the file was read appear in the sheet and the README: the TOA5 shift, rows left out, repeated days, trace rain, and rain that only ever rises, which looks like a running total.

**Gaps.** DSSAT, APSIM and AquaCrop stop on a missing value, so their files get a gap-free copy. It runs from the first to the last day with both Tmax and Tmin; when that is shorter than the chosen dates, the status line and READMEs give the dates. Values no station reports are taken as missing first, in every model file and table: rain, radiation, humidity, vapour pressure or wind below 0, radiation above 50 MJ/m², a temperature outside −60…60 °C, and Tmax = Tmin = 0 (an empty row). Then Tmax, Tmin and wind are linear between neighbouring days and reach up to 3 days past the first or last value; a longer run of wind at either end takes that calendar month's mean over the days written (the overall mean if the month has no wind), counted separately in the README. A column is left out only when the weather has none of it. Solar radiation is 0.16 √(Tmax − Tmin) Ra (FAO-56 Eq. 50), rain 0, vapour pressure from the dew point or Tmin, and ET₀ FAO-56 from the other columns; a Tmax below Tmin is swapped. Each README counts the filled days. SWAT+ (−99), EPIC (999) and APEX (blank or 9999) get their missing-value codes and generate those days.

**TAV and AMP.** Monthly means of (Tmax + Tmin)/2 need 20 or more days each. DSSAT pools all years (TAV = mean of the 12 means, AMP = warmest minus coldest month, as CSM, WEATHER.CDE and the official headers use it). APSIM averages the same two numbers over complete calendar years (its `tav_amp`). When the chosen dates do not cover every month, the app reads the last 10 complete years from the same source (gridMET for your own file). If that read fails, TAV and AMP come from the months the dates have and are marked approximate in the file and its README.

**Weather-generator statistics.** EPIC/APEX `.WP1` follow WXPM3020 (wet day > 0 mm; they match its output for the official TX1136 example within 0.001). SWAT+ `weather-wgn.cli`: wet day ≥ 0.1 mm; the maximum half-hour rain is a third of each month's largest daily rain, averaged over the years in which the month is complete (no sub-daily data). Each group of statistics (temperature, rain, solar radiation, humidity, wind) comes from the chosen dates when every calendar month has 20 or more days of it, pooled over the years; else from the 10 complete years (read whenever the dates lack a month or a variable) when they have 20 or more; else from the chosen dates with 10 or more days in every month; else from the 10 years with 10 or more, the last two with a note naming the variable and months. Otherwise the model's files are not written (`NOT_WRITTEN.txt` says why), rather than written with zeros. Monthly rain totals and wet days are per whole month, from the days with data. P(W|D), P(W|W) and SWAT+'s wet_dry and wet_wet use only days whose day before has data; with no such day, the month's share of wet days.

**Wind height.** DSSAT and APSIM: 2 m (DSSAT WIND in km/day); SWAT+: 1.7 m, u₁₀ × 0.17^0.2; EPIC/APEX: 10 m. EPIC/APEX `.WND` take the monthly speed and 16-direction frequencies from the app's hourly wind; months it does not cover use the daily 10 m wind, with directions spread evenly.

**File names.** APSIM and AquaCrop files are named from the coordinates to 0.001° (about 100 m), e.g. `WG33578N101855W`, so nearby sites do not overwrite each other; EPIC and APEX want 8.3 names, so theirs are W + 7 letters and digits packed from the same coordinates. The DSSAT station code is a letter, a digit and two letters or digits made the same way.

**CENTURY 4.0 and 4.5.** Formats come from the model's own Fortran readers (`wthini.f`, `weathr.f`, `sitein.f`, `ckdata.f`), which win where the manual differs, and were checked with test copies of those READ statements; the 4.5 reader is known only from a third-party copy with NREL's change list behind it; Century 4.6 is not covered, as no format document for it was found. The files have not been run in the model. DayCent, the daily version, takes other files: see below. When the site file cannot be made and weather is in the download, the `.wth` is kept and `site_NOT_WRITTEN.txt` says why; values the cleaner set aside are counted in the README, as for the other models.
- *`NAME.wth`:* whole calendar years, three lines a year (prec, tmin, tmax), `format(a4,6x,12f7.0)`, 94 characters a line, no header, one line end after the last line (a blank line stops the run). Rain in cm a month, the valid days' total scaled to the whole month; tmin and tmax the mean of the days having both. A month with under 20 valid days, or outside the dates, is −99.99: the model takes TMN2M and TMX2M from the site file, and draws rain at random from PRECIP, PRCSTD and PRCSKW. Both temperatures are flagged together, as 4.5 stops on a month with tmin above tmax.
- *`NAME.100`:* one file for each version (240 lines each), because the model stops at the first label it does not expect: 4.5 adds ROCK, PRECRO and FRACRO, drops W1LIG, W2LIG and W3LIG, and gives EPNFS another meaning.
- *Climate lines:* FILE100's formulas: mean, population variance (dividing by n) and skew from it, on the monthly values; they reproduce NREL's own oakridge.100 in 11 of 12 months. From the chosen dates when each month has 10 years, as the manual asks, else from 10 complete years read for them, else with what there is and a note.
- *Soil:* SAND, SILT, CLAY, BULKD, PH and ROCK are one value each for the top 20 cm of mineral soil, as thickness-weighted means, since CENTURY has one texture per soil. Surface O horizons, and any without sand and clay, are left out and named, unless O horizons with data reach past 20 cm from the surface (counting O horizons without data above them, and a merged custom layer that starts with O under 20 cm of O); then the top 20 cm of organic material is used, and the README says the values are not mineral soil's. A gap between horizons is split between the two; a soil that ends above 20 cm gives means over what there is, and the README gives the depths used. Water layers follow ADEP in fix.100, assumed to be NREL's 15, 15, 15, 15, 30, 30, 30, 30, 0, 0 cm: AWILT and AFIEL thickness-weighted per layer with SWFLAG 0, and AFIEL at least AWILT + 0.01, as the water routine divides by the difference. NLAYER is the nearest boundary to the soil's thickness by the Workbook (to a restriction, else the greater of 60 cm and the base of the Bt), at most 8, because ADEP(9) is 0; a restriction in what was left out above does not count. Custom depths are averaged onto ADEP's layers, and the README says so. DRAIN from the drainage class by the Workbook's table.
- *Soil C:* b = Σ (OM/1.724) × BD × thickness × 100 g C/m² over the top 20 cm, split 0.03, 0.44, 0.53 among the active, slow and passive pools by the Workbook's table (IVAUTO 0). Without organic matter across those 20 cm, IVAUTO 1 starts from the Burke equation, unless it gives under 500 g C/m² (4.0 has no floor and goes negative on dry warm sites), when the pools start from 500.
- *Our choices, as no source gives them:* the thickness weighting; the 20-day threshold; SSURGO's water values rather than the model's own equations; surface active C of 10 and litter of 100 g/m², the values the model itself starts from; and the plant and nutrient lines from NREL's Colorado shortgrass sample (c3grs.100), which the README says to review.

**DayCent (December 2016).** For NREL's DailyDayCent of 21 December 2016, the download its page calls DayCent 4.7 (`js/daycent.js`). Formats come from that package's readers (`getwth.f`, `sitein.f`, `ckdata.f`, `initlyrs.c`, `setlyrs.c`, `initsite_tg.c`), which win where the manual of May 2018 differs, and each file was checked field by field with Python copies of those checks against NREL's Wooster run of the same package and its Oak Ridge, Harvard Forest and Duke runs. The files have not been run in the model. The schedule, `fix.100`, `outfiles.in` and the other event files are yours; the README gives the lines they need. When the site file cannot be made and weather is in the download, the `.wth` is kept and `site_NOT_WRITTEN.txt` says why.
- *`DNAME.wth`:* D + the same 7 letters and digits as CENTURY's name. One row a day, free format: day, month, year, day of the year, Tmax and Tmin (°C), rain (cm), and, when every row has them, solar radiation (langley/day = MJ/m² × 23.9006), relative humidity (%) and 2 m wind (mph = m/s × 2.23694). The model checks each row's month and day of the year against its own calendar, sets leap years from the year field, and goes back to the file's start at its end, so the file runs from 1 January of the first year to 31 December of the last: days outside the dates are −99.99, which the model fills with TMX2M and TMN2M and, for rain, 0 (so they are dry). A day missing Tmax or Tmin has both flagged; a Tmax below Tmin is swapped. With the extra columns `sitepar.in` gets `usexdrvrs 1`; without them (any day missing one, including padded days, since the model stops on a flagged driver) 7 columns and `usexdrvrs 0`, and the README asks for FWLOSS(4) = 0.8 in `fix.100`, as NREL does.
- *`NAME.100`:* 245 lines in DayCent's order: CENTURY 4.5's lines with the slow pool as a surface and a soil pool, SOM2CI(1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2), and RCES2 for each; same labels as NREL's Oak Ridge, Harvard Forest and Duke files. Climate lines, soil C and the rest as for CENTURY, with the soil C in the soil slow pool and the surface one empty, SWFLAG and STORMF 0 as NREL's DayCent instructions ask, and BASEF 0.3 from NREL's Wooster run. DayCent replaces SAND, SILT, CLAY, BULKD, PH, AWILT and AFIEL with width-weighted means of `soils.in` (texture over the first CENTURY layer, 0–10 cm), so the site file holds those same means. PRECIP feeds N deposition and TMN2M, TMX2M flagged days; PRCSTD and PRCSKW are not used. TMN2M ≤ TMX2M is checked, as the model stops otherwise.
- *`soils.in`:* 13 columns a row (top, bottom, bulk density, field capacity, wilting point, evaporation coefficient, root fraction, sand, clay, organic matter, deltamin, Ksat in cm/s, pH). CENTURY layers follow ADEP 10, 20, 15, 15, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30, 30 cm (NREL's for DayCent), and the model stops unless each boundary down to NLAYER is a row's bottom, compared exactly, so depths are whole cm. NLAYER is the nearest boundary to the soil's depth (to its last horizon with water retention and bulk density), at most 9 (210 cm), as the model sets a larger NLAYER to 9; deeper soil is left out, and rows past a shallower soil's end repeat its deepest horizon. "Each model's usual layers" gives the rows of NREL's examples (0–2, 2–5, 5–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–45 … 180–210 cm; the first three are its advice for trace gases); the horizons as they are and custom depths are split at 2 and 5 cm and the ADEP boundaries. The model takes at most 20 rows (it stops on a 21st), so more are merged, the thinnest pair inside one CENTURY layer first. Each row is the thickness-weighted mean of what it covers, organic matter by mass and Ksat harmonic (cm/s = mm/h ÷ 36,000), field capacity at least wilting point + 0.01; missing Ksat from Saxton & Rawls, organic matter 0.01 and pH 6.3, each counted in the README. Root fractions are those of NREL's Wooster and Oak Ridge files spread by depth (none below 105 cm or below a root-restrictive layer, unless it is at the top) and scaled to sum to 1; evaporation coefficients 0.8, 0.2, 0 and deltamin 0.09, 0.07, 0.05, 0.02 in the top rows are Wooster's, deltamin at most 0.8 × the wilting point so that the model's minimum water stays above 0.
- *`sitepar.in`:* NREL's file of the same package, keyword by keyword in its order, which the model's reader needs (older versions read it by position). Changed: `usexdrvrs` from the weather file, and tbotmn, tbotmx, the soil temperature range below the profile, for which no source gives a rule: the mean of the climate lines' monthly air temperatures ± half their yearly range × exp(−(z + 5)/d), z the profile's depth and d = √(2κ/ω) = 147 cm from the model's thermal diffusivity κ = 0.00215 cm²/s. Cloud cover is NREL's Wooster values, as placeholders; the README says so.
- *Our choices, as no source gives them:* as for CENTURY, plus the depth rule, the root fractions by depth, deltamin, the padding of partial years, and tbotmn and tbotmx.

**CO₂ (APSIM).** NOAA GML Mauna Loa annual means 1959–2025 (the 1959 value before that), then +2.5 ppm a year (AquaCrop's assumption). Without a co2 column APSIM Next Gen assumes 350 ppm. DSSAT and AquaCrop read their own Mauna Loa files.

**Elevation.** The download waits for the page's lookup, at most 25 s from when the sheet was opened, then takes NASA POWER's cell, then the survey's value, else it is unknown (DSSAT's .WTH −99; SWAT+, EPIC and APEX 0; gridMET's humidity at sea-level pressure). The top README has an Elevation line.

**A weather file used elsewhere.** It belongs to the place it was loaded at: at another location the sheet goes back to gridMET, with the file one click away and its distance stated in the sheet and README, and it is chosen again back at its own location.

**Your soil file** (CSV, text or Excel; the sheet makes a template in the same way, with examples or this location's survey layers as its rows) is read by name in the same way, with lab spellings (Upper/Lower depth, From/To, Sand total, SandPct, pH water, pHw, pH 1:1, pH (H2O), EC 1:1, ECe, Sat. paste EC, CaCO3 equivalent, CCE, CEC7, WP, FC, 15 bar, 1/3 bar, −1500 kPa, −33 kPa, and SSURGO's `sandtotal_r`, `wthirdbar_r` and the like). Not read: pH in CaCl₂, KCl or buffer, fine-sand fractions, base saturation, ECEC, fragments by weight, and `LL` unless a `DUL` column stands next to it (elsewhere it is the liquid limit). The unit in a header is converted (depth in mm, in, ft or m; g/kg; Ksat in cm/h, cm/d, mm/d, m/d, µm/s or in/h; EC in µS/cm or mS/m); water contents above 1 are read as percent, and a column at most 1 beside them only if as a fraction it would break wilting point < field capacity < saturation (a sand's WP 0.9, FC 6). By weight, `FC (% by weight)` is multiplied by bulk density. Sand and clay of at most 1 are fractions. `<0.1` is half the limit and `>200` the limit; a unit in the cells counts as the column's; a code such as 999 is missing unless the unit can hold it (EC 999 µS/cm), with a note. `% Saturation` alone is a salinity lab's and not used; beside WP or FC it is water by volume. A row with no depths (a Mean) is left out; a layer without texture or bottom is refused when others lie below it; layers that overlap mean more than one profile and are refused; values no soil has (BD outside 0.02–2.7, Ksat above 5,000 mm/h, EC above 300) are refused with the layer named. A blank organic matter stays blank where a file can do without (each README says what that model does), as does a blank CaCO₃ (DSSAT −99), while a given 0 stays 0; and estimates take at most 1 % and no more than the layer above. Organic carbon gives organic matter × 1.724 when there is no organic matter column. Sand and clay that do not fit (below 0, or above 100 % together) are refused with the layer named. The sheet names the columns it did not use.

**Soil.** The survey component's horizons (or your table) in the common structure of `js/models.js`. Horizons count from the first with water retention and bulk density to the first without (bedrock or a cemented pan ends the profile).
- *Layers:* "each model's usual" splits the horizons at DSSAT's standard depths (5, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210 cm) and APSoil's 150, 150, 300 … mm layers. "The horizons as they are" writes them unchanged. In any layering, a model that takes fewer layers merges the thinnest neighbours below the surface layer until they fit (DSSAT 20, EPIC 15, APEX and SWAT's user soil table 10, AquaCrop 5): by thickness, with organic matter, organic carbon, CaCO₃ and total N by mass, as below, and Ksat as a harmonic mean. "Custom depths" re-layers the profile: each layer value = Σ wᵢxᵢ / Σ wᵢ over the horizons it overlaps by wᵢ cm, and Ksat = Σ wᵢ / Σ (wᵢ/Kᵢ); organic matter, organic carbon, CaCO₃ and total N, fractions of the fine earth by mass, take wᵢ × BDᵢ (or wᵢ where no horizon has a bulk density), so a thin peat over mineral soil keeps its mass of carbon (exactly where the horizons' coarse fragments are alike, as BD is the fine earth's). APSoil's standard layers and the area grid's layers are made the same way. Depths past a profile that simply stops repeat the deepest horizon; depths past bedrock are cut there. The root growth factor is recomputed for each layer.
- *DSSAT `WG.SOL`:* soil ID WG + the 8-digit component key for an unedited survey soil on DSSAT's usual layers; any other layering, an edited surface or your own soil gets WG + 8 letters and digits made from the written soil table, so a profile with other layers or values never takes an earlier profile's ID (DSSAT uses the first profile in WG.SOL with a matching ID); columns as in §4, values right-aligned in 6-character slots of at most 5 characters (SSKS with fewer decimals when large); a second tier with CaCO₃ (g/kg) on the same rows.
- *APSIM `.apsimx`:* version 168 JSON; LL15, DUL, SAT from the survey with SAT ≤ 1 − BD/2.65 − 0.001; AirDry = 0.5 × LL15 in the top layer; KS = Ksat × 24 mm/day; Carbon = OM/1.724; crop KL 0.06 to 0.01 with depth, XF 1 (0 below a restrictive layer); SWCON 0.3; initial water at DUL; NO₃ 1 ppm, NH₄ 0.1 ppm; summer from 1 April in the northern hemisphere.
- *AquaCrop `.SOL`:* SAT, FC, WP in vol %; Ksat mm/day; CN by hydrologic group A 46, B 61, C 72, D 77 (Table 2.14d, 5% initial abstraction); REW = round(10 (FC − WP/2) × 0.04), 0–15; CRa and CRb from the soil class (Tables 2.24s-3, 2.24s-5); gravel 0 because the survey's water contents are for the whole soil; penetrability 100%, or 0 below a restrictive layer.
- *EPIC/APEX `.SOL`:* moist albedo = 0.625 × dry; field capacity and wilting point on a fine-earth basis, θ / (1 − fragment volume); blank pH as 7.
- *SWAT+ `soils.sol` and QSWAT+ table:* horizons without a survey bulk density are left out and named in a note, as in the official SWAT+ soil database (not custom layers, which are your depths and always have a weighted bulk density); depths in mm, carbon = OM/1.72, rock = % by weight > 2 mm, albedo = dry albedo, USLE K = the survey's rock-free K, or for your own layers K = f_csand f_cl-si f_orgc f_hisand (Williams 1995, SWAT theory 4:1.1); texture up to the 16 characters SWAT+ reads.

**Figures and tables.** Charts are redrawn off screen at the chosen size: pixels = inches × DPI, rounded to a whole pixel on each axis, fonts in points (1 pt = DPI/72 px). PNG gets a pHYs chunk, JPEG its JFIF density, and TIFF is written in the app (baseline RGB, PackBits, resolution tags in inches). CSV files carry a UTF-8 byte-order mark and the units in the headers. The annual wind summary marks partial years ("Complete year", and "Hours with data in the period" as a % of the year); the monthly summary writes "not ranked (part of a year)" for the months of a period that does not cover all 12 and "no data" for months without data, and heads its shares "% of the year" or "% of the months with data". The plain CSV has a README too, giving the ET₀ source and the layers. Each model folder's README gives the layers its soil file has (count, depths, bottoms).

**Units.** `js/units.js` holds one table of quantities, each with a base unit, choices, factors and decimals, plus metric, US and default presets. Display values convert from the base; classes stay defined in their source units (E and I in t/ac/yr, NRCS specifications in US units) and their edges are converted for display; model files ignore it and use each model's units.
