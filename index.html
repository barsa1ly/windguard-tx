Wind Erodibility Groups: what the classes mean

NRCS sorts surface soils into nine Wind Erodibility Groups (WEG) by how easily they blow when bare and dry: 1, 2, 3, 4, 4L, 5, 6, 7 and 8. Group 1 is the most erodible and group 8 is not erodible. Each group has an erodibility index I: the tons of soil per acre per year a wide, bare, smooth field would lose in the climate of Garden City, Kansas. I runs from 0 to 310; groups 3, 4 and 4L share I = 86, and 4L holds calcareous (limy) loamy soils.

· For a named soil, NRCS uses the group recorded in the soil survey; the texture guide is for soils that are not identified (National Agronomy Manual, Part 502). So a soil's group can differ from what its texture suggests: Pullman clay loam is WEG 7. Rock fragments move a soil to a less erodible group: one group at 15–35% by volume, two at 35–60%, I = 0 above 60% (sands and loamy sands move less). Texture guide: USDA-NRCS National Soil Survey Handbook, Part 618 Subpart B, §618.93 (2024). “Calcareous” means the soil fizzes strongly in dilute acid (free lime).
